Eng-Unite

Eng-Unite was a student-led initiative founded in October 2025 to strengthen community, collaboration, networking and progression opportunities among engineering students across TU Dublin. Ceased in February 2026.

"Turning ideas into actions."

About Eng-Unite

Eng-Unite was created to bring students together across different years, course codes and engineering disciplines. The initiative began with a team consisting of myself and four volunteers and developed through workshops, guest speakers, networking opportunities, engineering discussions and collaborative activities.

The idea developed from my experience progressing from first year into second year of TU705. I wanted to recreate the strong community spirit that had developed within my own year and extend that environment to students across other stages and disciplines.

Eng-Unite primarily focused on TU705, TU706 and TU714 students across Years 1–3 before expanding to students from a wider range of engineering disciplines and TU Dublin campuses.

Mission & Purpose

The purpose of Eng-Unite is to encourage students to progress through their studies by creating a stronger sense of community, collaboration and support.

My Role — President & Founder

Founded: October 2025

Position: President

Team: Founder + four volunteers

I founded Eng-Unite after seeing the positive effect that strong peer relationships, motivation and community had within my own year. I wanted to extend that environment to students across different years, course codes and engineering disciplines.

As President, I managed a team of four volunteers and led the development and organisation of activities, events and opportunities for students.

Key Responsibilities

  • Organising student and industry events
  • Communicating with guest speakers and external contacts
  • Searching for new opportunities for students
  • Delegating tasks across the volunteer team
  • Training and supporting volunteers
  • Hosting team meetings
  • Brainstorming and developing new ideas
  • Preparing presentations and training material
  • Identifying target audiences
  • Public speaking and event hosting
  • Managing event logistics and room requirements
  • Maintaining communication before and after events

An important part of my leadership was supporting volunteers around their own college, personal and assignment commitments. Where circumstances changed, responsibilities could be re-delegated so that the team could continue operating without unnecessarily overloading individual members.

NASA Event — A Practical Leadership Example

The NASA aerospace event took place during spring break, when students were not normally on campus for classes. Despite travel disruption in Dublin on the day, including fuel protests and diversions, approximately 50 students attended and arrived on time.

The event brought students together from across TU Dublin campuses and generated positive feedback from students following the event. TU Dublin subsequently published an article documenting the event and identifying me as the primary organiser.
